# Research findings

Everything here was checked on 29–30 October 2026. Prices and benchmarks change, so check again before relying on a number.

## 1. What a Cloudflare scraping setup needs, and what Scrapling covers

The owner started with a checklist from Gemini of what scraping behind Cloudflare requires. We checked it against Scrapling's documentation (version 0.5).

**Scrapling has:**
- **Stealth browser:** `StealthyFetcher` / `StealthySession`, Playwright with Patchright stealth patches. It can drive the bundled Chromium, your installed Chrome (`real_chrome=True`), or a remote browser over `cdp_url`.
- **Cloudflare solving:** `solve_cloudflare=True` handles Turnstile and the "Just a moment" interstitial (managed, interactive and invisible). The docs say the timeout should be at least 60 s.
- **TLS and header matching:** the HTTP `Fetcher` impersonates a browser's TLS fingerprint (`impersonate=`, via curl_cffi), sends realistic headers (`stealthy_headers`), and offers HTTP/3. The browser generates a real user agent for its version.
- **Session persistence:** sessions keep cookies and the same fingerprint. `save_state`/`load_state` stores cookies, local storage and IndexedDB as JSON. This doesn't work together with `proxy_rotator`.
- **Fingerprint and leak protection:** headless and CDP leaks are patched, `hide_canvas` adds canvas noise, `block_webrtc` stops WebRTC leaks, `dns_over_https` stops DNS leaks, and `timezone_id` and `locale` are settable.
- **Proxies:** `ProxyRotator` (cyclic, or a custom strategy), a per-request proxy override, and blocked-request retries in spiders.
- **Crawl control:** Spiders offer concurrency limits, `download_delay`, AutoThrottle (backs off on blocks, honours `Retry-After`), pause and resume, and a cache.
- **Parsing:** CSS, XPath, text and regex search, `find_similar`, and adaptive selectors that survive small redesigns.
- **API discovery:** `record_requests=True` records the page's background XHR and fetch calls.

**Scrapling doesn't have:**
- Residential or mobile proxies themselves. You buy those separately.
- Human-like mouse paths or typing delays. You'd script those in `page_action`.
- Random timing jitter (our scripts add it).
- Any documented AudioContext, WebGL, screen or font spoofing. Running `real_chrome` on a real Mac avoids most mismatches.
- An automatic hand-off of the Cloudflare pass to the plain HTTP fetcher. `6.py` does this itself.

Google's page cache, one of the alternatives on Gemini's list, was shut down in 2024. The Wayback Machine still exists.

## 2. Proxies

**What a proxy does:** the site sees the proxy's IP, not yours. *Rotating* means a new IP per request or every few minutes. *Sticky* means the same IP for a set time. For Cloudflare, use sticky: the pass cookie (`cf_clearance`) is tied to the IP that earned it, so switching IPs means a new check, which the owner saw take about 45 s.

**Home internet first:** the owner's own connection is already a residential IP. At low volume, around one request every 90 s, that's fine on its own. Paid residential proxies are for volume, or for running from a server.

**Provider choice, from Proxyway's independent *Proxy Market Research 2026*, which tested 13 providers:**
- **Decodo:** 99.92% success globally, the most unique US IPs (~961K), and about 80% success on hard targets. Prices are mid-range. **This is the one bought.**
- **DataImpulse and Evomi:** the cheapest, around $0.50–1/GB, but about half the IPs in their standard pools were already flagged (49.6% and 50.9%). That's bad for Cloudflare, which checks IP reputation first.
- **Oxylabs and Bright Data:** similar quality to Decodo, but $4–8/GB with higher minimums.
- No benchmark tested Cloudflare specifically.

**Proxidize:** searching for Decodo showed Proxidize's site, because Proxidize publishes "Decodo vs Proxidize" pages and ads. They are **different companies**. Proxidize residential costs $1/GB as a monthly plan from 25 GB, with data that rolls over. It wasn't in Proxyway's test, a May 2026 Trustpilot review says many of its "residential" ISPs aren't home connections, and reviewers mention an ID check after payment. Proxidize mobile is $2/GB from 25 GB, and that data doesn't roll over. Nothing was bought there.

## 3. Data and cost estimates for ~130,000 text lookups

These are estimates, not measurements. The first real `6.py` run measures the true figure.

| How each lookup is done | Data per lookup | Total | Rough Decodo cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full browser for every page | 1–3 MB | 130–390 GB | $325–975 |
| Browser, skipping images and fonts | 0.3–1 MB | 40–130 GB | $110–350 |
| **Browser for the Cloudflare pass, plain text for the pages** (what `6.py` does) | ~0.03–0.3 MB | ~4–40 GB | ~$16–160 |
| The site's own data feed, if it has one | ~0.02–0.05 MB | 3–7 GB | $12–28 |

How to measure: run `6.py` with `LIMIT = 50`, read MB used in Decodo → Usage statistics, and multiply by about 2,600 (130,000 ÷ 50). Remember that the first page on each IP costs extra, because of the browser and the Cloudflare check.

## 4. Speed

- **First visit per IP:** up to ~45 s for the Cloudflare check, as the owner saw on the row-3 page.
- **Later pages on the same IP as plain text:** roughly 1–2 s each.
- **Pauses:** `6.py` waits 2–5 s between lookups for each browser. With 2 browsers, that's very roughly 0.4–1 lookups per second. At that rate, 130,000 lookups take somewhere between ~1.5 and ~4 days of running. More browsers means faster, with more IPs in use at once.
- **Request rate on a home IP:** one request every 90 s is about 1,000 a day and looks like a person refreshing. Every 10 s is where blocks and repeated challenges become likely.
